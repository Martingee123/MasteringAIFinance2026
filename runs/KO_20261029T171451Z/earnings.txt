L17: ATLANTA, Oct. 27, 2026
L18: – The Coca-Cola Company today reported third quarter 2026 results. “Our results this quarter demonstrate our system’s sustained momentum, giving us confidence in our updated full year guidance,” said Henrique Braun, CEO of The Coca-Cola Company. “By staying close to consumers and executing with agility across our global system, we are delivering quality growth while continuing to invest in the capabilities, brands and people that will fuel our future.”
L19: Highlights
L20: Quarterly Performance
L21: •
L22: Revenues:
L23: Net revenues grew 7% to $13.4 billion, and organic revenues (non-GAAP) grew 7%, driven by a 7% increase in concentrate sales and 1% growth in price/mix. Concentrate sales were 2 points ahead of unit case volume due to the timing of concentrate shipments.
L24: •
L25: Operating margin:
L26: Operating margin was 33.4% versus 32.0% in the prior year, and comparable operating margin (non-GAAP) was 32.9% versus 31.9% in the prior year. Operating margin performance included items impacting comparability, as well as currency tailwinds. Comparable operating margin (non-GAAP) expansion was driven by organic revenue (non-GAAP) growth and currency tailwinds, partially offset by an increase in marketing investments and higher operating expenses.
L27: •
L28: Earnings per share:
L29: EPS grew 8% to $0.93, and comparable EPS (non-GAAP) grew 11% to $0.91. Both EPS and comparable EPS (non-GAAP) performance included the impact of a 2-point currency tailwind.
L30: •
L31: Market share:
L32: The company gained value share in total nonalcoholic ready-to-drink (“NARTD”) beverages.
L33: •
L34: Cash flow:
L35: Year-to-date cash flow from operations and free cash flow (non-GAAP) were $12.5 billion and $11.4 billion, respectively.

L37: Company Updates
L38: •
L39: Celebrating local heritage and strengthening communities through its brands:
L40: Recently, the company reached milestone anniversaries in three important markets: the United States, Mexico and Nigeria. In the United States, the Coca-Cola system marked 140 years of serving consumers and strengthening communities. According to a recent study from Steward Redqueen, the system today supports nearly 1 million jobs throughout the value chain and contributes approximately $85 billion in annual economic activity in the United States. From Trademark Coca-Cola to fairlife, the company’s brands continue to provide consumers with beverages across many drinking occasions. The system celebrated 100 years of growing together with Mexico through a campaign rooted in consumers’ deep, multigenerational connections with Coca-Cola. The campaign came to life through limited-edition packaging, local cultural icons and integrated experiences, generating strong consumer engagement that contributed to volume growth during the quarter. In Nigeria, a 75-year anniversary celebration of Coca-Cola’s connection with generations of Nigerians brought together national pride, music, authentic connection and self-expression through distinctive packaging, creator content and experiential activations. The campaign reached more than 100 million consumers, contributing to volume growth and value and volume share gains for the quarter. Together, these milestones demonstrate how the company combines global scale with deep local roots to create meaningful consumer experiences and drive growth.
L41: •
L42: Globally connecting brands to consumers through locally relevant music experiences:
L43: The company continues to connect its brands to passion points, with music serving as a powerful global platform to drive relevance and consumer engagement. As an example, across Europe, Coca-Cola and Sprite were highlighted at 60 festivals in more than 20 markets, reaching over 12 million attendees and over 500 million livestream viewers while building direct consumer connections through first-party data. Highlights include uplifting activations at Tomorrowland in Belgium, Rock in Rio Lisboa in Portugal and Coca-Cola Music Experience in Spain. The company drove strong local momentum in Greece by offering Trademark Coca-Cola products in the newly launched 500ml “Supercan” across nearly 20 live music events. In Great Britain, the company bridged music and soccer by partnering with British rapper Central Cee to create an original song for its English Premier League campaign. Together, these activations strengthened brand relevance and contributed to volume growth for the quarter in Europe.

L1068: The company reports its financial results in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States (“GAAP” or referred to herein as “reported”). To supplement our consolidated financial statements reported on a GAAP basis, we provide the following non-GAAP financial measures: “comparable net revenues,” “comparable currency neutral net revenues,” “organic revenues,” “comparable operating margin,” “underlying operating margin,” “comparable operating income,” “comparable currency neutral operating income,” “comparable EPS,” “comparable currency neutral EPS,” “comparable currency neutral EPS excluding acquisitions and divestitures,” “underlying effective tax rate” and “free cash flow,” each of which is defined below. Management believes these non-GAAP financial measures provide investors with additional meaningful financial information that should be considered when assessing our underlying business performance and trends. Further, management believes these non-GAAP financial measures also enhance investors’ ability to compare period-to-period financial results. Non-GAAP financial measures should be viewed in addition to, and not as an alternative for, the company’s reported results prepared in accordance with GAAP. Our non-GAAP financial measures do not represent a comprehensive basis of accounting. Therefore, our non-GAAP financial measures may not be comparable to similarly titled measures reported by other companies. Reconciliations of each of these non-GAAP financial measures to GAAP information are also included below. Management uses these non-GAAP financial measures in making financial, operating, compensation and planning decisions and in evaluating the company’s performance. Disclosing these non-GAAP financial measures allows investors and management to view our operating results excluding the impact of items that are not reflective of the underlying operating performance.

L1071: “Currency neutral operating results” are determined by dividing or multiplying, as appropriate, our current period actual U.S. dollar operating results by the current period actual exchange rates (that include the impact of current period currency hedging activities) to derive our current period local currency operating results. We then multiply or divide, as appropriate, the derived current period local currency operating results by the foreign currency exchange rates (that also include the impact of the comparable prior period currency hedging activities) used to translate the company’s financial statements in the comparable prior year period to determine what the current period U.S. dollar operating results would have been if the foreign currency exchange rates had not changed from the comparable prior year period.

We generally refer to acquisitions and divestitures of bottling operations as “structural changes,” which are a component of acquisitions and divestitures and also include the impact of intercompany transactions between our operating segments, as applicable. Typically, structural changes do not impact the company’s unit case volume on a consolidated basis or at the geographic operating segment level.
